Now measure words (量詞):

English doesn't have Chinese-style classifiers, but uses "unit nouns + of". The pattern: 數詞/限定詞 + 單位名詞 + of + 名詞.

Available unit nouns: cup, glass, piece (E), bottle, bowl (E), bit (E) — but "a bit" is banned ("a bit" → "a little"). So avoid bit as measure word. Also "piece" is E layer.

Also "half of the work" — half + of. "half" is a quantifier not exactly measure word but uses of.

Also "part of the work" — part (E).

Patterns:
- a cup of tea / coffee
- a glass of water / milk
- a piece of paper / bread / wood / cake
- a bottle of water / milk / oil / juice
- a bowl of soup / rice
- 2 cups of tea
- half of the work / half of the money

Note: 不可數名詞 (uncountable nouns) like water, milk, tea, bread, money, rice can't take "a" directly or plural -s; you need the measure word. This is a key teaching point for Chinese speakers (who use 個、杯、瓶 classifiers): English measure words are only needed for uncountable things, and the unit noun carries the plural.
